# India's Current Trade Situation (Deep Dive)

**Date: 2026-02-09**
**Confidence Score: Medium**

## BLUF (Ketsuron)
India's trade strategy shows positive momentum from domestic policies like the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes and strong growth in the services sector. However, the recent US-India trade deal is viewed by experts as a "harm reduction" measure rather than a transformative growth driver, offering only a temporary competitive edge. Structural challenges persist, highlighted by a widening trade deficit that contrasts with the performance of manufacturing-led peers like Vietnam, indicating that long-term sustainability depends on securing more binding international market access and diversifying exports beyond the service sector.

## What We Know
*   **Policy Impact:** Government initiatives like "Make in India" and PLI schemes have successfully attracted significant investment commitments (US$20-21 billion) and boosted domestic production value to over US$162 billion. (Source: Kiban Agent)
*   **Services Sector Strength:** The 11% YoY growth in services exports is primarily driven by Software Services, which account for over 40% of the total, and Professional & Management Consulting, which grew at an average of 25.9% between FY23-FY25. (Source: Shoko Agent)
*   **Competitive Landscape:** In contrast to India's widening trade deficit (reaching -$21.8B in Oct 2025), Vietnam has maintained a significant trade surplus ($20.03B cumulative in 2025), largely due to its strong manufacturing base and export of processed goods. (Source: Hikaku Agent)
*   **Expert Outlook on US Deal:** The consensus is that the February 2026 US deal, which lowers tariffs to 18%, provides a fragile, short-term advantage. Skepticism surrounds India's $500B purchase pledge (seen as "aspirational"), and economic models still project a potential net negative GDP impact of ~0.71% from the overall trade environment. (Source: Kenkai Agent)

## What We Do Not Know
*   **Granular Export Data:** The specific dollar-value contribution of high-growth service sub-sectors (like Software and Consulting) was not available, making it difficult to assess their full economic weight.
*   **Binding Deal Details:** The full, legally binding text of the US-India trade agreement has not been made public, leaving the long-term impact and enforcement mechanisms uncertain.
*   **Global Comparative Blindspots:** The available data for comparison with Mexico and Vietnam was heavily skewed towards trade with the US, limiting a comprehensive global analysis of competitiveness.

## Practical Next Steps
*   **Monitor Final Deal Text:** The primary task is to analyze the legally binding text of the US-India trade deal upon its release to understand firm commitments versus aspirational goals.
*   **Track Manufacturing Exports:** Closely monitor export data for the 14 PLI sectors to verify if domestic production gains are translating into increased global market share against competitors.
*   **Analyze Services Diversification:** Investigate whether the rapid growth in services is diversifying beyond software and consulting into other high-value areas.
