# India's Current Trade Situation

**Date: 2026-02-09**
**Confidence Score: Medium**

## BLUF (Ketsuron)
India's merchandise trade balance remains in deficit, reported at -$25.04 billion in December 2025, with a widening current account deficit. Key exports include services, textiles, and pharmaceuticals, while major imports are mineral fuels, precious stones, and machinery. A recent US-India trade deal in February 2026 is expected to reduce tariffs and impact bilateral trade dynamics.

## Evidence (Shōko)
*   Merchandise trade deficit: -$25.04 billion (December 2025), widened from -$24.53 billion (November 2025). [4, 7]
*   Current Account Deficit (CAD): Widened to ~2.8% of GDP in Q4 2025; projected to reach $37 billion (1% of GDP) in 2026 due to rising non-oil/non-gold imports. [1]
*   Exports: Surged 19.37% year-on-year to $38.13 billion in November 2025. [4]
    *   Major exports include services (growing 11% YoY in 2025), textiles, pharmaceuticals, gems and jewellery, leather, machinery, and agriculture. [1, 2, 3]
    *   Exports to the US: $86.35 billion in 2024 (FY25 similar), boosted by tariff cuts. [2, 3, 5]
*   Imports: Rose 8.8% year-on-year to $63.6 billion in December 2025. [4]
    *   Major imports include mineral fuels and oils, precious/semi-precious stones and metals, nuclear reactors/machinery, and electrical equipment. [3, 4]
    *   Imports from the US: $48 billion in FY25, with a new deal framework targeting $500 billion over 5 years. [3, 5]
*   Recent US-India trade deal (February 2026): Reduces US tariffs on Indian goods to 18% (from 25-50%), expected to boost Indian GDP by 0.2 points. [1, 2, 3, 6]
*   Bilateral trade with US: Hit $132 billion in FY25, with India holding a $40.82 billion surplus pre-deal. [3]

## Synthesis (Bunseki)
India's trade landscape in late 2025 and early 2026 is characterized by persistent merchandise trade and current account deficits, primarily driven by imports of energy and gold. However, robust growth in services exports and a significant new trade agreement with the US aim to mitigate these pressures. The tariff reductions from the US deal are anticipated to enhance India's export competitiveness, potentially shifting the bilateral trade balance with the US from a surplus to a deficit for India in the long term, as outlined in the new $500 billion trade framework.
